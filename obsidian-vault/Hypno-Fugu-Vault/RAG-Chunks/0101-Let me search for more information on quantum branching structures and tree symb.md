IX. Tree of Life Symbolism Across Civilizations
The tree of life symbol appears independently across cultures dating to the 4th millennium BCE in Mesopotamia, spreading to Persia, Egypt, India, China, Mesoamerica, Norse mythology, and Abrahamic religions. Common features include: an axis connecting heaven, earth, and underworld; representation of the source of life; a cycle of life and death. In Kabbalah, the tree of life is depicted as ten interconnected nodes—a branching network structure. While these symbolic convergences are striking, they likely reflect universal human observation of actual tree branching rather than knowledge of a deeper principle. The biological reality of branching optimization may have inspired the symbolic convergence, but the symbols should not be taken as evidence of the mathematical principle.
