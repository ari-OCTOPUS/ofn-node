# Accounting (AU) — Research Digest 2026-07-04 (live kickoff)

## Today's question
For an Australian sole trader / solo operator, what are the key ATO and ASIC deadlines, thresholds and concessions for FY2025–26, and which bookkeeping tools best automate the tax/GST admin?

## Findings

- **A sole trader must lodge an income tax return even if income is below the $18,200 tax‑free threshold — there is no separate threshold for business income; self‑lodgers are due 31 October (2 Nov 2026 as 31 Oct 2026 falls on a Saturday), with a later date if you use a registered tax agent.** For bookkeeping this means the business books must be reconciled and P&L‑ready by October regardless of how small the turnover is; the $18,200 tax‑free threshold applies to the individual's total taxable income, not as an exemption from lodging. *(General info — the exact due date and whether you get an agent extension depend on your registration; confirm your own dates in myGov / with your agent.)* [source](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/reports-and-returns/income-tax-return)

- **GST registration is compulsory once GST turnover reaches $75,000 (rolling 12‑month test, current + projected); below that it's optional.** Register within 21 days of crossing the threshold. Once registered you charge 10% GST, claim GST credits, and must lodge a BAS. Practically: watch a rolling 12‑month turnover figure in your bookkeeping tool, not just the July–June financial year, so a mid‑year spike doesn't leave you unregistered. *(General info — voluntary registration under $75k is a choice with trade‑offs; not personal advice.)* [source](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst)

- **Quarterly BAS due dates for FY2025–26 are Q1 (Jul–Sep) 28 Oct 2025, Q2 (Oct–Dec) 28 Feb 2026, Q3 (Jan–Mar) 28 Apr 2026, Q4 (Apr–Jun) 28 Jul 2026 — with roughly 2 extra weeks if you lodge the BAS online/electronically.** This is the core recurring compliance rhythm for a GST‑registered sole trader; PAYG instalments (if you're in that system) usually ride on the same activity statement. Set recurring reminders ~2 weeks before each date so GST is set aside, not scrambled for. *(General info — dates shift for annual/monthly reporters and can be affected by public holidays; check your own activity statement.)* [source](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/preparing-lodging-and-paying/business-activity-statements-bas/due-dates-for-lodging-and-paying-your-bas)

- **You enter the PAYG instalments system automatically once your latest return shows instalment (business/investment) income of $4,000+ and tax payable of $1,000+ on your notice of assessment; instalments are then paid quarterly (generally 28 days after quarter‑end).** These are pre‑payments of the year's income tax, offset against the final bill at return time — so they're a cash‑flow item, not an extra tax. Budget for them alongside GST so the quarterly outflow isn't a surprise. *(General info — you can vary instalments if income drops, but under‑varying can attract interest; not personal advice.)* [source](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/income-deductions-and-concessions/payg-instalments)

- **The $20,000 instant asset write‑off is confirmed law for FY2025–26 (assets first used or installed ready for use between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026), for businesses with aggregated turnover under $10 million, applied per asset.** A solo operator can immediately deduct eligible tools/equipment/tech under $20k each rather than depreciating them over years — useful for end‑of‑year tax planning, but only worthwhile if the purchase is genuinely needed for the business. *(General info — legislated via the Treasury Laws Amendment (2025) measure; eligibility and GST‑exclusive vs inclusive cost tests apply. Not personal advice.)* [source](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/small-business-newsroom/20000-instant-asset-write-off-for-2025-26)

- **A registered business name (if trading under anything other than your own legal name) is an ASIC obligation that must be renewed every 1 or 3 years, and ASIC's business‑name fees are CPI‑indexed each 1 July.** Missing renewal can lead to the name being deregistered, so it belongs on the annual admin calendar next to tax dates. Note this is separate from your ABN (free, via the ABR) — the two are different registrations. *(General info — exact current fee is set on ASIC's fee schedule and updates annually; check the live figure before paying.)* [source](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/payments-fees-and-invoices/asic-fees/fees-for-commonly-lodged-documents/business-names/)

- **For automating the admin, the solo‑operator tools split into two shapes: full accounting software (Xero, MYOB, incl. the mobile‑first MYOB Solo ~$11–12/mo, Reckon One, Rounded) that you drive yourself, vs. a done‑for‑you tax service — Hnry — which deducts income tax and GST at the point each invoice is paid and handles BAS/return filing, charging ~1% + GST per payment capped at $1,500 + GST/yr.** For a very low‑admin sole trader who dislikes bookkeeping, Hnry trades a per‑payment fee for not touching a ledger; for someone wanting control, ownership of data, or growth toward employees, Xero/MYOB fit better. *(General info from vendor/comparison sources — pricing and features change; verify current plans directly and consider your own volume before choosing.)* [source](https://rounded.com.au/blog/myob-vs-xero)
