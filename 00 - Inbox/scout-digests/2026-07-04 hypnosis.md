# Hypnosis & self-awareness — Research Digest 2026-07-04 (live kickoff)

## Today's question
When self-directed techniques (self-hypnosis, HRV biofeedback, interoception training) actually change how people feel, is that a *specific* effect of the technique — or largely expectation, relaxation, and attention? What does the 2023–2026 evidence say, and where is it still thin?

## Findings
- **[peer-reviewed]** **Remote HRV-biofeedback shows a medium effect on depression and on HRV itself** — a 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis (18 studies, 1,352 participants) reported g ≈ −0.41 for depressive symptoms and g ≈ +0.44 for HRV vs. controls, suggesting the resonance-breathing mechanism (slow ~6 breaths/min paced breathing) does more than just relax people. Effects on anxiety/stress were less consistent. [source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10484-025-09750-w)
- **[peer-reviewed]** **Hypnosis reduced fibromyalgia pain in a blindly-evaluated RCT** — 49 participants, 8 weekly 1-hour sessions with analgesia suggestions vs. an unscripted-conversation control; the hypnosis arm significantly lowered pain scores at post-intervention and at follow-up. Blinded outcome assessment strengthens the case that the effect isn't purely the therapist's attention. [source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S155083072400123X)
- **[peer-reviewed]** **Neurofeedback's benefit shrinks under proper sham controls** — 2024–2025 meta-analyses (attention, PTSD, depression) find effects are notably larger with *passive* controls (waitlist / treatment-as-usual) than with *active* sham/yoked-sham comparators; several sham-controlled contrasts were not significant. Design, not just the intervention, drives reported efficacy — the placebo debate is live. [source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2025.1658652/full)
- **[peer-reviewed | mechanism, inconsistent]** **Hypnosis is linked to Default Mode Network / salience / executive-network shifts, but findings don't fully replicate** — a 2024 review reports altered anterior DMN activity and connectivity changes tied to changed "self" awareness in high-hypnotizables, while explicitly flagging that non-standardized inductions and designs make the neuroimaging literature inconsistent. Treat the brain "mechanism" story as suggestive, not settled. [source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10886478/)
- **[peer-reviewed]** **An app-delivered self-hypnosis tool improved stress management in an RCT** — a 2025 *npj Digital Medicine* randomized trial of a self-hypnotic relaxation app reported benefits for stress/anxiety, indicating self-administered (no live clinician) protocols can move outcomes — relevant for scalable self-practice, though app trials are prone to expectancy effects. [source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-02182-0)
- **[peer-reviewed]** **Real-time heartbeat feedback measurably sharpens interoceptive accuracy — modality matters** — a 2024 RCT (n=60) found a single session of *haptic* (not visual) heartbeat feedback raised interoceptive accuracy and confidence and shifted attention toward the body; separate 2025 trials of 5-day biofeedback and short body-scan practice likewise improved heartbeat detection and MAIA body-awareness subscales. Interoceptive "self-awareness" is trainable, but gains are often small and task-specific. [source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/psyp.14648)

## Spores (open questions for next run)
- Do self-hypnosis and HRV-biofeedback gains *transfer* to daily life and persist at 3–6 month follow-up, or fade once the guided practice stops?
- Is "interoceptive accuracy" (heartbeat-detection tasks) the right target, given ongoing critique that these tasks partly measure heart-rate knowledge/beliefs rather than true bodily sensing — and how does that reframe self-awareness training?
