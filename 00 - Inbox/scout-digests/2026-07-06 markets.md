## Findings

- **RBA held the cash rate at 4.35% on 16 June 2026 (next decision 11 Aug), but this is a *hiking* cycle, not the cutting cycle usually assumed for 2026 — three rate rises since the start of the year were driven by a Middle East oil-supply shock feeding through into headline and underlying inflation, which the Board still judges "too high."** The Board is explicitly watching whether the oil-driven inflation impulse becomes embedded, not looking for room to cut. → *For cash decisions:* AU-dollar savings/HISA yields have no near-term reason to fall; there's no rate-cut tailwind pulling Ari's cash toward risk assets domestically the way US cut-hope is doing for BTC. [RBA statement](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-15.html)

- **AUD/USD is sitting near a three-month low (~US$0.69) despite the local hiking cycle, because Australia just posted a AUD 3.02bn May trade deficit — the largest since December 2015 — while the US dollar stays broadly firm on resilient US data.** Rate hikes alone aren't enough to support the currency when the trade balance and terms of trade turn against it. → *For portfolio math:* any BTC/USD gains currently convert to *more* AUD than they would in a strong-AUD regime — a live one for the eToro portfolio's AUD-denominated accounting; conversely imported hardware (mining rig parts, ESP32/Orange Pi units) costs more in AUD right now. [AUD/USD data](https://tradingeconomics.com/australia/currency)

- **ASX 200 and global risk sentiment are choppy and headline-driven rather than a clean risk-on trend: a tech-led Wall Street sell-off (S&P −0.22%, Nasdaq −0.66%, copper −1.5%, WTI −2.1%) was followed within days by an ASX 200 jump of 1.4% to 8,844 on easing Middle East tension and the reported reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, with gains capped by rate/bond-yield worries tied to the RBA's own hiking stance.** → *For sizing:* this is not a "risk appetite is back, deploy capital" environment — it's a market re-pricing a live geopolitical/oil-supply risk on a rolling basis, arguing for the same small, capacity-matched positioning already used elsewhere in the vault (P11 pattern). [ASX outlook](https://www.forex.com/en-us/news-and-analysis/asx-200-market-outlook-ri<REDACTED-OPENAI-KEY>/)

- **Gold and iron ore are moving in opposite directions, an unusual pairing worth flagging: gold is near US$4,170/oz (off its Jan-2026 record intraday spike above US$5,500, but J.P. Morgan still projects US$6,000 by year-end) on safe-haven/rate-cut-hope demand, while iron ore has fallen to a 10-month low near US$98–99/t on weak Chinese demand.** Gold-up + iron-ore-down together look like a stagflation-flavoured fear signature (inflation hedge bid, AU's key export commodity soft) rather than a simple risk-on/risk-off read, and it independently explains part of the AUD's weakness (soft iron ore = weaker AU terms of trade). [Gold](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/gold-prices-today-thursday-july-2-2026-prices-break-above-4100-following-a-disappointing-june-jobs-report-120224733.html) · [Iron ore](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/iron-ore)

- **BTC (~US$61.8k, +3% week, still deep in its 2026 drawdown) is rallying tactically on weak US jobs data reviving Fed-cut hope — but a same-week analysis argues the old 2022–2023 "Bitcoin = leveraged rate bet" correlation has structurally broken in 2026: ETF flows, corporate treasury holdings and EM currency-hedge buyers have built an "inelastic demand floor" that no longer moves cleanly with rate-cut odds.** BTC still popped on the July 3 weak-jobs print, so the old reflex hasn't vanished, but the claim is it's now one input among several rather than the dominant driver. → *For the crypto thesis:* this reinforces yesterday's crypto-scout finding that on-chain regime metrics (MVRV-Z ≈0.2, SOPR ≈0.98 — both in "accumulation" territory) are a more load-bearing signal right now than chasing Fed-meeting headlines; macro is context, not the trigger.
