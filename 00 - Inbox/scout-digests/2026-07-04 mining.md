# Mining — Research Digest 2026-07-04 (live kickoff)

## Today's question
For a low-power RK3588 (Orange Pi 5 Pro) rig plus ESP32 helpers in 2026, which algorithm gives the best real hashrate-per-watt, and which candidate coins are alive enough not to die under us?

## Findings
- **Algorithm choice dominates the RK3588's economics: RandomX (Monero) tops out near ~1000 H/s on an RK3588 board, while VerusHash hits ~6.6–6.7 MH/s at only ~9 W wall power.** On the same silicon VerusHash is orders of magnitude more work-per-joule than RandomX because it fits the ARM cache/instruction profile far better. If the rig's goal is efficiency (H/s per watt), lead with VerusHash on the Orange Pi and treat RandomX/XMR as the "hold the node + occasional CPU" play, not the workhorse. Baseline: a Rock 5B (also RK3588) averaged ~728 H/s on RandomX. [source](https://cryptomining-blog.com/tag/rockchip-rk3588s-veruscoin-hashrate/)
- **DERO (AstroBWT/v3) is the third viable ASIC-resistant ARM target: ~1.34 KH/s at ~10 W on a Pi 5, with a miner now ARM-memory-optimized.** AstroBWT is CPU-only by design (BWT core has resisted 30 years of GPU/FPGA attempts), so an RK3588 should land in a similar efficiency band to the Pi 5 figure. Worth a bench slot alongside VerusHash to compare live payout, not just raw hashrate. [source](https://www.hashrate.no/cpus/pi5/DERO)
- **ESP32 as a miner is a novelty, not a contributor: real-world Monero output is ~14 H/s (users report), versus a theoretical ~400 H/s ceiling that nobody reaches.** Pool mining on ESP32 is described as "extremely unprofitable"; the only non-zero-EV framing is solo "lottery ticket" hashing against a local node. Decision: keep ESP32s for sensing/telemetry/orchestration around the rig, not for hashrate. Don't budget any expected revenue to them. [source](https://coinbuzznow.com/news/esp32-monero-mining-hashrate-exploration/)
- **CPU-mining in 2026 has effectively three ASIC-resistant lanes — RandomX (XMR), AstroBWT (DERO), VerusHash (VRSC) — and XMR only pays if power is under ~$0.08/kWh.** Above that, home XMR returns go marginal, which matters because RK3588 hashrate is low; the rig's edge has to come from cheap/near-free power + high-efficiency algo, not from out-hashing anyone. This directly favors the VerusHash-first strategy for our hardware. [source](https://laikalabs.ai/market-intelligence/best-cpu-mining-coins)
- **Death-watch is real and worsening: 53%+ of all listed crypto tokens are now dead, Q4 2025 alone killed ~7.7M tokens (post Oct-10 liquidation cascade), and a "dead coin" = <$1,000 trade volume over 3 months.** For a small CPU rig this is the core existential risk: mining a thin coin means the payout can evaporate mid-run. Screen any candidate for active dev + non-trivial 90-day volume before pointing hashrate at it. [source](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/01/14/more-than-half-of-all-crypto-tokens-have-failed-and-most-died-in-2025)
- **Concrete cautionary signal: the Kadena Foundation announced (Oct 2025) it would cease operations "unable to continue supporting the blockchain's development," and KDA fell 60%+ in a day.** Even a formerly top-tier chain with a foundation can pull the plug — so treat "small community RandomX/Verus forks with low difficulty" (tempting for easy blocks) as high death-risk. Prefer coins with independent, still-funded development over easy-difficulty forks. [source](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/01/14/more-than-half-of-all-crypto-tokens-have-failed-and-most-died-in-2025)
