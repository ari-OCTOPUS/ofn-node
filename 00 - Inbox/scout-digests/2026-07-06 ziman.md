# Ziman (gallery/branding) — Research Digest 2026-07-06

## Today's question
Following yesterday's spore #1: which *single* Sydney market best fits a Bloom rose-gold handmade gift/homeware brand for a monthly first-channel test — comparing **actual stall fees, insurance requirements, and application friction** at The Rocks, Kirribilli, Paddington, Glebe, and Sydney Boutique Markets (Rouse Hill)? *(Yesterday established "monthly stall OR Etsy"; today prices the stall option concretely.)*

## Findings

- **Kirribilli Markets is the cheapest low-friction entry: ~A$140–150 all-in per market day.** Verified from the official stallholder page: open-air stalls (Bowling Green/Gravel) **$120**, Burton St Tunnel (artisan-made products only, by application) **$140**; compulsory public liability insurance is sold at booking for **$20 per market**; table hire $10, marquee $50. Bookings are online-only in quarterly windows (Jul–Sep 2026 opened 1 June), non-refundable, all-weather. A small fee increase took effect 26 May 2026, so current numbers are fresh. **What this means for Ziman:** a full monthly test costs roughly **$150/market with zero insurance paperwork** (buy the $20 cover at checkout) — the lowest-commitment way to put Bloom in front of real buyers; the artisan-only Tunnel is also brand-aligned positioning if accepted. [source](https://www.kirribillimarkets.com/stallholder-information/)

- **The Rocks Markets is the premium option but is effectively gated right now: applications are CLOSED, assessment takes ~8 weeks when open.** Official FAQ (verified): standard 2.4×2.4m stall **$147/day**, 1.8×1.8m **$82/day**, 3×3m **$229/day** (GST incl.); ABN **required**; a committee assesses against selection criteria (brand identity, uniqueness, sustainability) and wants website/social links + quality product and stall photos; marquee/trestle provided. Trades Fri–Sun weekly. **What this means for Ziman:** treat The Rocks as the *aspirational second channel*, not the first test — the closed applications + 8-week committee cycle conflict with testing now, and its criteria (established brand presence, photos of an existing stall setup) are easier to satisfy *after* a few Kirribilli/Glebe outings. Its requirement list doubles as Bloom's brand-asset checklist. [source](https://www.therocks.com/stallholder-faqs)

- **Glebe Markets: general stall from ~$135 + $20 insurance surcharge if you have no policy; Paddington: application-vetted, fee ~A$92 per third-party listing (not published officially).** Glebe adds $20 to the stall fee if you lack your own public/product liability cover and hires tables at $11 — a Saturday inner-west crowd (younger, vintage/creative). Paddington (Saturdays, Uniting Church-run) applies via an online form judged on product images and "brand ethos"; fees aren't public — the A$92 figure comes from an aggregator and needs confirming on application. **What this means for Ziman:** both are viable Saturday alternatives in the $110–155/day band; Paddington's design-led reputation fits a rose-gold gift brand, but its opaque fees and vetting make Kirribilli the faster start. [source](https://glebemarkets.com.au/apply-for-a-stall) · [source](https://www.paddingtonmarkets.com.au/stallholder-info)

- **Sydney Boutique Markets (Rouse Hill Town Centre) is the only *natively monthly* option — first Sunday of each month — and explicitly courts "fashion, accessories, homewares & more from emerging designers."** 2026 stallholder applications are open (via ABME's application portal); fees aren't published and come with the application. The retail-centre setting (Main Street, Rouse Hill) draws a Hills-district family/gift shopper rather than tourists. **What this means for Ziman:** its once-a-month cadence *exactly* matches a capacity-ceiling-bounded production cycle (make for 4 weeks → sell 1 day), and the homewares/gift positioning is the closest category fit of all five — worth applying in parallel while booking Kirribilli. [source](https://www.sydneyboutiquemarkets.com.au/)

- **Insurance economics flip at ~3 market days per year.** Casual single-day stallholder cover in NSW runs **from ~$35–60/day ($10M cover)**; annual maker policies run **from ~$124–229/year** (AAMI from $124, MyMarketInsurance $179 for $10M incl. product liability, AUZi handmade policies also cover market + online sales). Markets on council land commonly demand $20M. **What this means for Ziman:** for a genuinely monthly test (12 days/year), an **annual policy (~$180) beats per-market fees (~$240+/yr)** and removes a barrier at every market that doesn't sell cover at checkout (Glebe, Paddington, Rouse Hill) — one small fixed cost that unlocks every channel including future retail pop-ups. [source](https://mymarketinsurance.com.au/stall-holders/) · [source](https://www.auzi.com/portal/public-liability-insurance-market-stall/)

**Synthesised recommendation (propose-only):** First test = **Kirribilli Markets** (~$150 all-in, online booking, insurance at checkout, artisan Tunnel positioning) **plus a parallel application to Sydney Boutique Markets Rouse Hill** (monthly cadence + best category fit). Budget frame for the whole experiment: ~**$150–170/market day** (stall + insurance + table) — so a 3-month test ≈ **$450–500 total**, a concrete number the capacity/margin math (still blocked on the units/week figure) must beat. Defer The Rocks until Bloom has stall photos and a live social presence; note ABN is required there (and for Etsy above A$75k) — flag to Accounting.
