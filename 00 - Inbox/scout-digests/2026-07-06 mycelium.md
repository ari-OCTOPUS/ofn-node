# Mycelium (nature → AGI) — Research Digest 2026-07-06

## Today's question
Closing yesterday's first spore: is there a single, unified biological rule — one decay constant plus usage-triggered reinforcement — that lets high-value traces persist while low-signal ones fade automatically, or does nature actually need two separate "keep" and "forget" rules? What does the real answer imply for deciding when a scout-digest should silently age out versus get promoted to a permanent `07 - Knowledge` note?

## Findings

- **Ant trails really do run on one knob, not two.** The classic pheromone-trail model (Deneubourg-lineage, formalized in continuous foraging models) updates trail strength with a single rule: constant evaporation multiplied by strength, plus a deposit that scales with how many ants just walked it (and, in refined versions, with path *quality* — shorter/better paths get more deposit per ant, not just more ants). A popular, good trail gets topped up faster than it evaporates; an abandoned trail just fades — no separate "forget" rule is needed. [source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.5611) — **What this teaches our architecture:** our existing evaporation policy (status: inbox → archived after >14 days, per the Mycorrhizal Map's P3 pattern) doesn't need a bolt-on "promotion" rule. Treat every wikilink/citation *into* a digest (from a nightly `synthesis`, another scout, or the Mycorrhizal Map ledger) as a pheromone deposit that resets/extends its TTL. A digest that keeps getting cited never crosses the archive threshold; one nobody references ages out on schedule. Promotion-worthiness = reinforcement rate > decay rate, observed over time — not a judgment call made once.

- **But forest soil carbon genuinely needs *two* distinct pools, not one tunable rate — a real counter-example, not just an untested metaphor.** Soil organic carbon splits into a labile pool (turnover: days to months) and a stable pool protected by aggregation or mineral association (turnover: decades to centuries); these aren't the same material at different reinforcement levels, they are structurally different substrates. [source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772826925000720) Mycorrhizal fungi actively convert labile inputs into mineral-associated stable carbon via a distinct physical/chemical binding step, not just by "leaving it alone longer." [source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0038071722000359) — **What this teaches our architecture:** don't over-generalize the "one decay knob" finding above. `07 - Knowledge` notes shouldn't be modeled as "a digest that merely survived long enough" — they should require a distinct, qualitative promotion step (owner review + rewrite into a canonical note, our existing §4c decision-tree gate) analogous to the mineral-binding step, not an automatic threshold-crossing. Keep the two-tier design (ephemeral inbox litter vs. deliberately-bound knowledge) rather than collapsing it into pure decay math.

- **Neuroscience splits the difference, and its split is the more useful template: one continuous decay, plus a separate, qualitatively distinct "capture" event.** In synaptic tagging-and-capture, ordinary activity leaves a decaying molecular "tag" at a synapse; the tag only becomes a *stable* long-term change if a separate, stronger novelty/reward-linked protein-synthesis signal arrives at that synapse within a limited time window — mere repetition of the same weak stimulus doesn't trigger capture. [source](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.05.08.084053v1.full) · [source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ejn.14902) — **What this teaches our architecture:** the trigger for promoting a digest shouldn't be raw link-count (repetition) alone. It should require a qualitatively distinct "capture" signal we already produce elsewhere in the fleet — e.g. the Mycorrhizal Map's rule that a pattern needs to recur in **≥2 independent domains** before it's added to the pattern ledger (P1–P13). That cross-domain confirmation *is* our synaptic-capture event; plain same-scout repetition should just extend TTL, not promote.

- **This exact mechanism is being built for LLM agents right now — it's not a stretch metaphor, it's a live 2026 design pattern.** "Oblivion" (arXiv, Jan 2026 preprint) frames agent forgetting as *decay-driven loss of accessibility*, never explicit deletion: a read path decides when to consult memory, a separate write path reinforces only the memories that actually contributed to a response, and everything else fades in retrieval priority but stays reactivatable if conditions change. [source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00131) — **What this teaches our architecture:** this is close to a direct spec for our digest lifecycle. Never hard-delete an inbox digest (`.agentignore`/dedup already forbids destructive cleanup); "archived" should mean *lower retrieval priority*, not gone — a query or a future scout can still surface it if a topic resurfaces. Reinforcement should be tied to whether a digest's *content* got used (cited in a synthesis, fed into a promoted knowledge note), not whether it was merely reopened or reread.

## Spores (open questions for tomorrow)
- **Operationalizing "capture":** right now cross-domain confirmation (≥2 scouts) is judged by whoever writes the nightly synthesis/consolidator by hand. Can this be made a checkable, near-automatic signal (e.g., the Mycorrhizal Map ledger literally counting independent-domain citations per pattern) without turning into the same over-eager "positive-claims-only" citation bias that inflated the mother-tree kin-provisioning myth (2026-07-05 finding)?
- **Substrate, not just schedule:** the soil-carbon counter-example suggests real permanence comes from binding to a different, more structural substrate (mineral association) — not just surviving longer. Should a promoted `07 - Knowledge` note be required to sit inside the existing wikilink graph at some minimum "structural centrality" (linked from a PROJECT.md or MOC), rather than just being a digest that resisted archival the longest?
