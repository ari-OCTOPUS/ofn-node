# Project-F — Research Digest 2026-07-06

## Today's question
For a single-creator subscription-content business, what free-to-paid conversion and monthly churn rates count as "healthy" vs "needs work" in 2025–2026 — and what moves each metric most?

## Findings

- **The real median free-to-paid conversion is 0.62% — far below the folklore "2–5%".** A June 2026 first-party analysis of thousands of paid publications found ~6 payers per 1,000 free subscribers at the median; the top 25% reach roughly 2–5%, and top-decile finance/investing publications hit 18–20% — a 22–26x spread inside the same vertical, driven by offer design, not list size. For Project-F: treat ~0.6% as the starting baseline, 2–5% as the "healthy" target, and don't judge the business a failure at 1%. [source](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026)

- **Healthy monthly churn for paid content sits around 5–8%; above ~12% is a leak.** Category medians run from 5.06% monthly churn (≈20-month subscriber lifetime) at the best to 16.67% (≈6-month lifetime) at the worst — a ~3x difference in revenue per subscriber before pricing even enters. Fast-moving, crowded niches churn worst (AI content: 13.33%/mo). For Project-F: benchmark ≤6%/mo as healthy, 6–10% as workable, >12% as the top priority to fix before spending anything on acquisition. [source](https://pressgazette.co.uk/newsletters/newsletters-2026-prices-retention-churn/)

- **Price point filters churn: members paying under ~$7/mo churn at roughly double the rate of those paying $10–15/mo.** Community-pricing data shows the ~$12/mo tier maximizes revenue per visitor — cheap tiers convert more but churn out in ~4.5 months, while $25+/mo halves conversion without enough per-member revenue to compensate. Involuntary churn (failed payments) adds another 20–40% on top of voluntary churn. For Project-F: the industry default of ~$10/mo (steady since 2024) is a floor, not a ceiling — avoid under-pricing to "convert more". [source](https://paprika.bot/blog/paid-community-pricing/), [source](https://pressgazette.co.uk/newsletters/newsletters-2026-prices-retention-churn/)

- **Annual billing is the single highest-leverage retention move: annual subscribers churn at roughly half the rate (≈8.5%/yr vs ≈16%/yr month-to-month) and are ~4–5x less likely to cancel in the first 12 months.** The 2026 industry consensus is to launch with an annual plan at a 15–20% discount from day one, not add it later — it removes 11 of 12 monthly "should I cancel?" decision points and cuts failed-payment churn. For Project-F: make annual-with-discount the default presented option. [source](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026), [source](https://growtoro.com/blog/paid-newsletter-conversion-free-to-paid-playbook)

- **Dunning (failed-payment recovery) is nearly free money: email-only dunning recovers ~42% of failed payments, and full recovery systems reach up to ~70%.** Combined with decline-code-specific retry timing and card-updater tools, this attacks the 20–40% involuntary slice of churn without touching content at all. Short trials also beat long ones for conversion: ~39% trial-to-paid on 7-day trials vs <25% on 14-day in daily-content offers. For Project-F: verify the payment platform's retry/dunning settings are actually enabled — it's the cheapest churn fix available. [source](https://churnbuster.io/articles/how-to-reduce-customer-churn), [source](https://paprika.bot/blog/paid-community-pricing/)
