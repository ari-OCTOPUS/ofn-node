# Hypnosis & self-awareness — Research Digest 2026-07-06

## Today's question
Spore #1 from the 2026-07-04 digest asked whether self-hypnosis / mind–body gains *transfer and persist* once the guided practice stops. Today I follow only the **hypnosis** half of that (the HRV half was covered in today's `health` digest to avoid overlap): **when hypnotherapy works, does the effect actually last months-to-years after the course ends — and does self-administered practice hold up as well as therapist-led?**

## Findings

- **[peer-reviewed]** **A 2025 two-year follow-up shows gut-directed hypnotherapy (GDH) benefit is genuinely durable, not a short bump.** Based on articles retrieved from PubMed: Lövdahl et al. (2025, *United European Gastroenterology J*) followed 289 IBS patients through a 12-week nurse-administered program; responder rate was 64.3% post-treatment and essentially flat at 61.8% at 2 years, with symptom, anxiety and depression reductions holding (p < 0.001). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ueg2.70060) — *What this means:* durability is now the strongest evidence claim in this space; the effect survives long after the last session, which is exactly the "does it transfer" question the spore raised.

- **[peer-reviewed]** **Even self-administered / lower-intensity delivery keeps most of the effect.** The Lövdahl cohort found individual sessions predicted somewhat better response than group, but group hypnotherapy still sustained benefit at 2 years [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ueg2.70060); a 2024 Frontiers mini-review likewise reports app- and audio-delivered GDH sustaining responder gains at ~6 months, close to clinician-led rates. [source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1389911/full) — *What this means:* scalable, self-driven protocols (relevant to a solo practitioner) don't collapse relative to expensive 1-on-1 — a green light for the project's self-practice angle.

- **[peer-reviewed]** **Older long-horizon data agrees, but only for responders — the "who it sticks for" filter matters.** Per PubMed, long-term refractory-IBS follow-up work (Vidakovic-Vukic / Whorwell lineage) found that of patients who responded to a course, the large majority retained improvement years later (means of ~4–5 years). [source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22339617/) — *What this means:* persistence is conditional on being an initial responder; hypnotherapy looks more like "convert-then-durable" than "works-on-everyone."

- **[peer-reviewed | contested]** **For self-hypnosis in chronic pain, durability holds — but it often doesn't beat other active self-care at follow-up.** A randomized longitudinal chronic-pain trial (Liège; 4 arms incl. self-hypnosis/self-care) found a strong *time* effect (all groups improved and held at 6–12 months) but *no between-group* superiority for self-hypnosis specifically. [source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8345881/) A dedicated self-hypnosis chronic-pain RCT (NCT06279650) running its 3- and 6-month endpoints through 2024 is the one to watch. [source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06279650) — *What this means:* keep the epistemic guard tight — "self-hypnosis lasts" is supported, but "self-hypnosis is *better than* structured relaxation/self-care" is not yet demonstrated for pain; separate the two claims.

- **[peer-reviewed | contested]** **The responder trait itself may be partly movable, which reframes "who benefits."** Carleton Skills Training work shows some low-hypnotizables shift toward high after structured training; a 2023 Frontiers study, however, found mere repeated *experience*/online-vs-offline modality did **not** move hypnotizability — implying it's explicit skill training, not exposure, that matters (if anything does). [source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10936846/) — *What this means:* hypnotizability is less of a fixed ceiling than the classic "stable trait" story, but the evidence is mixed; treat trainability as a live hypothesis, not settled, before building any self-practice curriculum around it.
