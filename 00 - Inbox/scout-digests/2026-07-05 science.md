# Science (frontier → nature-inspired AGI) — Research Digest 2026-07-05

*First run of the `science` scout. No prior `*science*` digest exists, so all signal below is new. Topic deliberately steered clear of the `mycelium` scout's fungal-memory territory: this is the **physics/materials/neuro** frontier of learning-in-matter — machines with no processor and no backprop, where global function emerges from purely local rules. Same design principle nature uses, a different substrate.*

## Today's question
Can a **physical system learn a real task with no processor, no stored program, and no backpropagation** — updating only via *local* rules baked into its own matter — and if so, what does the physics of "matter that learns" teach a decentralized, memory-based multi-agent architecture about credit assignment, robustness, and *where computation should live*?

## Findings (today's spores)

- **A network of self-adjusting resistors now does reinforcement learning with no processor at all — and some RL machinery is *more* natural in physics than in code.** Dillavou & Mishra (Nov 2025) adapt Q-learning to *Contrastive Local Learning Networks* (CLLNs): analog nets of nonlinear resistors that are inherently low-power and robust to physical damage, previously only supervised. They solve two RL problems and find the policy and value functions map *naturally* onto the physics, while the **replay buffer** — a von-Neumann-style external memory store — is the awkward, unnatural piece to bolt on. [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17789) — **Pattern for our architecture:** where a component maps naturally onto the substrate, lean in; where you're forcing a *stored external buffer* onto a system that wants to compute-in-place, that's the expensive, brittle seam. Our replay-buffer analog is the note-store the fleet reads back — worth asking which of our "memory" ops are really *re-derivable in place* rather than needing a separate durable buffer, and which genuinely must be stored (cf. mycelium's dehydratable-spore thread).

- **Trained physical networks "become what they learn" — you can read a network's function off its perturbation response without knowing the task.** Stern, Guzman, Martins, Liu & Balasubramanian (final v. Apr 2025) prove that in resistor networks learning via local contrastive rules, the *physical* Hessian (curvature you can measure by poking the network with random perturbations) becomes directly coupled to the *cost* Hessian (what it was trained to do). The learned task carves a physical "crack" of low conductance; the network's response *reveals the function it was tuned to, with no knowledge of the task itself*. They tie this to conserved-but-idiosyncratic structure in proteins and brain circuits. [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09689) — **Pattern for our architecture:** learning leaves a *readable structural fingerprint*. For the fleet, this suggests a cheap "what has this scout actually learned?" probe — inspect the *shape* of a scout's accumulated digest-trace (which tags/links it stiffens around) instead of re-reading every note. Structure-reveals-function is a monitoring/eval primitive: audit the imprint, not the whole history.

- **Autonomous *generative* learning in matter: the Restricted Kirchhoff Machine learns a data distribution (unsupervised), not just input→output.** Guzman, Ciarella & Liu (Sept 2025) build a self-learning resistor circuit that mirrors a Restricted Boltzmann Machine — noise + rectifiers + bipartite architecture — and trains on binarized MNIST *without a processor or external memory*, with time/power/energy per operation scaling *better* than an RBM on CPU/GPU as the system grows. [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15842) — **Pattern for our architecture:** physical learning is escaping the narrow supervised box into *generative/probabilistic* territory, and its advantage *widens with scale*. The lesson isn't "buy analog hardware"; it's that **distributed local update rules can do the same work as a central optimizer and get cheaper per-unit as they scale** — the exact bet the fleet makes (many small local scouts > one big central reasoner). Watch this class for the moment it crosses from proof-of-concept to deployable substrate.

- **Self-organising memristive networks: global collective computation emerges from purely local nanoscale interactions — including dynamical phase transitions — with no explicit programming.** In these non-equilibrium networks, simple local plasticity at each junction propagates under network constraints into global self-organisation, and that interplay *is* the learning mechanism; the system reaches criticality-like transitions on its own. [source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.00747) — **Pattern for our architecture:** this is the hardware proof of the fleet's core biomimetic thesis — *local rule + shared constraint → emergent global function, no central planner*. It also flags **criticality** as the operating regime where these systems compute best. Open design question for us: is there a "phase-transition"/criticality knob for the fleet (scout density × frequency × coupling) that maximizes useful emergent signal before it tips into noise?

- **Imperfection is not a bug to eliminate — analog learning drifts, and that drift mirrors the brain's representational drift.** Dillavou, Guzman, Liu & Durian characterize how real CLLNs develop limit cycles, precision limits, and *memory erasure of previous tasks* driven by an "uncontrolled learning bias" that continuously rewrites the internal representation — explicitly *reminiscent of representational drift in the brain*. Rather than needing a companion digital model, they derive a system-agnostic training method that suppresses the harm while keeping the physics. [source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22887) — **Pattern for our architecture:** a decentralized learner that keeps updating will *drift and quietly forget* — our fleet's exact catastrophic-forgetting risk as digests accumulate and re-consolidate. The finding is double-edged and useful: (a) drift is *intrinsic* to local-rule learning, so plan for it (versioned/append-only traces, the evaporation-vs-hoard policy already open in mycelium's spores); (b) it's *tameable without a heavyweight central corrector* — a lightweight system-agnostic protocol beats a bolted-on digital twin.

## Spores (open questions for tomorrow)
- **Readable-imprint probe:** if a trained physical network's *function* is recoverable from its perturbation response without knowing the task (Stern et al.), what is the cheapest analog for a scout — a "poke" that reveals which tags/domains a scout has actually stiffened around — so the consolidator can audit *what was learned* from a structural fingerprint instead of re-reading every digest?
- **Where's our von-Neumann tax?** Physical learning collapses the compute/memory split — learning lives *in* the substrate. Which parts of our pipeline pay for separating the note-store from the reasoning, and what would "in-note" (in-substrate) learning look like — a digest that updates its own conclusions in place vs. a fresh run re-deriving them each time?
